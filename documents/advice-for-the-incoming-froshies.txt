# Associate advising
The author describes meeting a faculty advisor and one or more associate advisors during orientation to work out a first-semester schedule. An associate advisor can offer a student's perspective, while the faculty advisor helps with academic planning.

# Choosing calculus
The author suggests considering the 18.01A/18.02A sequence when a review of single-variable calculus would help. The post also describes moving to 18.01 if the faster format proves unsuitable.

# Chemistry options
The author describes 5.111 as introductory chemistry with more attention to liquid-state systems and 5.112 as an advanced option. The author personally enjoyed 3.091 for its focus on solid-state materials.

# Physics with a slower pace
The author recommends 8.01L to a student who felt underprepared in physics. The post describes it as covering the same material as 8.01 over more time in a lecture format.

# Advising formats
The author discusses traditional advising, advising seminars, and communities such as Terrascope. A credit-bearing seminar may be appealing, but the author says to budget time for its work alongside regular classes.
