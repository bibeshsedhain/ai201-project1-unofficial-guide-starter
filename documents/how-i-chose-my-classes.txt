# Three questions
The author sorts elective choices into three questions: whether the class fits their circumstances that semester, whether it will be enjoyable and engaging, and what they will gain from taking it.

# Schedule feasibility
The writer checks conflicts with lecture and exam times before enrolling. For lab subjects that require longer sessions in person, the author also looks for a continuous block of free time rather than counting only the units.

# Past students' experience
The author prefers asking someone who already took the course. When that is impractical, the author uses course evaluation scores out of seven as a starting point and also checks the syllabus and how much attention the instructor gives to teaching.

# Using campus resources
One reason the author chose some hardware courses was access to resources that would be hard to reproduce independently, including cleanroom equipment and quantum optics hardware. Individual feedback and instructor access also mattered.

# Manageable course load
Four to five classes were realistic for this author alongside sleep, food, and a social life. The post recommends considering workload, how a course counts toward graduation, and how regularly it is offered.
