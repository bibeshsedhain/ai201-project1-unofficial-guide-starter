# Starting schedule
CJ suggests starting with a science GIR such as 8.01 mechanics or 18.01 calculus, an introduction to a potential major, a humanities, arts, or social sciences class, and a fourth subject from one of those groups.

# Trying out subjects
CJ calls sampling classes before deciding what to keep class shopping. Because first-year students have a credit cap, the author says they may need to drop a subject before adding another. Visiting an opening lecture can help students hear the syllabus before committing.

# Prerequisite chains
CJ recommends using CourseRoad or degree charts to see which requirements unlock later subjects. Biological Engineering is the author's example of a major where a long chain of prerequisites makes looking ahead helpful.

# Friends in classes
The author likes taking a subject with friends because they can work on problem sets together and ask questions outside office hours. CJ's friends sometimes collect planned classes in a shared spreadsheet.

# Travel and lunch
CJ checks the earliest start time, the time available to get lunch, and how far apart consecutive classrooms are. The post uses a Sloan-to-W97 trip as an example of a walk that can take longer than the gap between classes.
